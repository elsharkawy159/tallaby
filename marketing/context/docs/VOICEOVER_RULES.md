# Egyptian Arabic Voiceover Rules

## 1. Core Objective

Whenever generating, rewriting, translating, or preparing a script for Arabic voiceover, use **natural Egyptian Arabic (العامية المصرية)** by default.

The voiceover must sound like a real Egyptian person talking naturally to another person.

The delivery should be:
- **Smooth:** Natural flow between words and sentences, without awkward pauses.
- **Bold:** Confident, persuasive, and energetic when the content calls for it.
- **Clear:** Every word must be understandable, with proper articulation and pronunciation.
- **Human:** Natural rhythm, conversational expressions, realistic emphasis, and appropriate emotional variation.
- **Engaging:** Capture attention quickly, maintain interest, and finish with a clear message or call to action.

Avoid robotic narration, exaggerated acting, stiff formal Arabic, unnatural sentence structures, and generic AI-generated advertising language.

These rules apply to all voiceover projects unless the user explicitly requests another dialect, language, or style.

## 2. Egyptian Dialect Is Mandatory

Write the script the way Egyptians actually speak in everyday life, especially in natural Egyptian advertising and social media videos.

Prefer familiar Egyptian expressions:
- إنت / إنتوا
- عايز / عايزة
- تقدر / تقدروا
- هتقدر
- دلوقتي
- كمان
- عشان
- علشان
- بدل ما
- معاك / معاكم
- خلّيك
- يلا
- بص
- تخيّل
- كل اللي عليك
- متضيّعش الفرصة
- الحق قبل ما العرض يخلص

Choose expressions that fit the audience, brand, and context. Do not force slang into every sentence.

### Use natural Egyptian word order and phrasing

Always prefer the expression that sounds most natural to an Egyptian listener, even when another construction is technically understandable.

Examples:

| Avoid | Prefer |
|---|---|
| كل مصر | مصر كلها |
| في جميع أنحاء مصر | في كل حتة في مصر / في مصر كلها |
| قم بالشراء الآن | اشتري دلوقتي |
| يمكنك الاستفادة من | تقدر تستفيد من |
| لا تفوّت هذه الفرصة | متضيّعش الفرصة دي |
| احصل على منتجك | اطلب منتجك |
| نوفر لك تجربة مميزة | بنوفرلك تجربة مميزة |
| هل تبحث عن طريقة سهلة؟ | بتدور على طريقة سهلة؟ |
| من أجل توفير الوقت | عشان توفّر وقتك |
| سوف تتمكن من | هتقدر |
| ابدأ رحلتك الآن | ابدأ دلوقتي / يلا ابدأ |

These are examples, not rigid replacements. Select the most natural phrase for the actual sentence.

## 3. Write for the Ear, Not the Eye

A voiceover script is meant to be heard, not read silently.

- Use short, conversational sentences.
- Express one main idea per sentence.
- Prefer familiar words over complicated vocabulary.
- Use natural transitions between ideas.
- Remove unnecessary introductions and repetitive statements.
- Avoid long sentences that force the speaker to rush or run out of breath.
- Rewrite any sentence that sounds awkward when spoken aloud.
- Keep the message clear even when the listener is distracted or watching on a mobile phone.

Read every script mentally as if a confident Egyptian presenter were speaking it.

If a sentence sounds like a formal written article, rewrite it into conversational Egyptian Arabic.

## 4. Natural Human Delivery

The generated voice must sound conversational, not like a newsreader or a traditional corporate advertisement.

### Pacing
- Use a moderate, comfortable speaking pace.
- Slow down slightly for important benefits, prices, offers, and key information.
- Allow short, natural pauses between distinct ideas.
- Avoid unnatural gaps between individual words.
- Never rush the final call to action.

### Intonation
- Vary the pitch and emphasis naturally throughout the script.
- Give the opening enough energy to capture attention.
- Use confident emphasis on the most important words.
- Use a warmer, more conversational tone when explaining benefits.
- Increase excitement when presenting a genuinely exciting offer.
- End with a clear, decisive call to action.

### Emotional delivery
Match the tone to the purpose:
- Sales advertisement: confident, persuasive, energetic.
- Brand introduction: professional, warm, trustworthy.
- Product demonstration: clear, enthusiastic, informative.
- Explainer: friendly, natural, easy to follow.
- Emotional storytelling: sincere, expressive, never melodramatic.
- Urgent offer: energetic and compelling without sounding aggressive.

Do not maintain the same pitch, intensity, or rhythm for the entire recording.

## 5. Voice Characteristics

Unless the user specifies otherwise, aim for:

- Natural Egyptian accent, recognizable to listeners across Egypt.
- Clear consonants and understandable vowels.
- A full, confident, well-supported voice.
- Smooth transitions between phrases.
- Natural breathing and sentence rhythm.
- Clean pronunciation without mumbling.
- Moderate energy with deliberate emphasis.
- Realistic emotional expression.

Avoid:
- Robotic or synthetic-sounding delivery.
- Excessively deep, artificial voices.
- Shouting or aggressive sales delivery.
- Excessive dramatic pauses.
- Overly exaggerated Egyptian slang.
- Flat, monotonous narration.
- Unnatural pronunciation of Arabic words, English brand names, or numbers.

The goal is a polished human performance, not an exaggerated character voice.

## 6. Script Structure for Advertisements

When writing advertising voiceovers, use this structure when appropriate:

1. **Hook:** Capture attention within the opening few seconds.
2. **Problem or desire:** Identify something the audience wants or struggles with.
3. **Solution:** Introduce the product, service, or offer naturally.
4. **Benefits:** Explain the most valuable benefits in clear, concrete language.
5. **Trust or reassurance:** Include a relevant reason to trust the offer when supported by the supplied information.
6. **Call to action:** Tell the listener exactly what to do next.

Do not force every script into this structure if a different format works better.

Prioritize clarity and persuasion over filling time.

Never invent discounts, guarantees, product specifications, customer testimonials, scarcity claims, or other facts that were not provided or verified.

## 7. Arabic Writing and Pronunciation

- Use Arabic script for Egyptian Arabic.
- Write colloquial words in their natural Egyptian forms.
- Add punctuation to help the voice model understand sentence boundaries.
- Use commas for short pauses and full stops for completed thoughts.
- Use question marks for genuine questions.
- Use ellipses sparingly; do not use them to create excessive pauses.
- Add diacritics only when they help resolve an important pronunciation ambiguity.
- Avoid excessive tashkeel, which can sometimes make synthetic voices sound unnatural.
- Keep the script clean and ready for direct voice generation.

### Numbers, prices, and abbreviations

Pay special attention to:
- Prices in EGP.
- Percentages and discounts.
- Measurements and product quantities.
- Dates, times, phone numbers, and URLs.
- English product names and brand names.

Write numbers in words when doing so improves pronunciation and flow. For prices, preserve the exact numerical value and currency.

For example:
- `٢٥٠ جنيه` → `ميتين وخمسين جنيه`
- `٥٠٪ خصم` → `خصم خمسين في المية`
- `٣ منتجات` → `تلات منتجات`

Choose the natural spoken form that fits the sentence. Never change the meaning, value, or unit.

For English brand names, provide a pronunciation hint only when the voice model needs it. Never change the official spelling of a brand in accompanying captions or metadata.

## 8. Advertising Language That Sounds Egyptian

Use natural persuasion rather than generic marketing clichés.

Prefer concrete benefits:
- هتوفر وقتك ومجهودك.
- كل حاجة هتلاقيها في مكان واحد.
- اطلب بسهولة ومن غير تعقيد.
- خلّي شغلك أسهل.
- كل اللي عليك إنك تبدأ.
- مستني إيه؟ ابدأ دلوقتي.

Use these only when relevant and factually accurate.

Avoid:
- Overly formal corporate language.
- Repeated phrases such as "في عالم مليء بالتحديات".
- Empty superlatives without evidence.
- Repeating the product name in every sentence.
- Unnatural translations of English advertising copy.
- Forced rhymes or exaggerated promises.

The script should feel written specifically for Egyptians, not translated from another language.

## 9. Punctuation and Voice Model Instructions

Prepare scripts so they work well with Higgsfield and other connected voiceover MCPs.

When the tool supports voice instructions, use a concise direction similar to:

"Native Egyptian Arabic accent. Natural conversational delivery. Smooth, confident, bold, and crystal-clear articulation. Human-like pacing, realistic intonation, subtle emotional variation, and natural pauses. Emphasize important benefits and the final call to action. Avoid robotic narration, exaggerated acting, shouting, and unnatural pauses."

Adapt these instructions to the actual capabilities and parameters of the connected tool.

If the tool supports separate fields, keep:
- Spoken script.
- Voice or style instructions.
- Pronunciation guidance.
- Optional timing or scene cues.

separate whenever possible.

Do not insert production notes such as `[pause]`, `[excited]`, or `[deep voice]` into the spoken script unless the specific tool supports those annotations and the user wants them.

If the tool supports voice selection, prefer a suitable native Egyptian voice over a generic Arabic voice. Do not assume every MCP supports the same controls.

## 10. Tool-Agnostic Generation Workflow

For every voiceover request:

1. Understand the target audience, platform, purpose, and desired duration.
2. Write the script in natural Egyptian Arabic.
3. Refine phrasing, word order, and expressions to ensure authentic Egyptian speech.
4. Remove unnecessary words and improve the opening hook.
5. Check every number, price, product claim, brand name, and call to action.
6. Add suitable voice and delivery instructions.
7. Select the best available connected voice generation tool based on its actual capabilities.
8. Generate the voiceover using Higgsfield or another connected MCP.
9. Review the generated result if audio playback or audio inspection is available.
10. Correct mispronunciations, unnatural pauses, flat delivery, and awkward sentence transitions, then regenerate when appropriate.

Do not claim that audio quality or pronunciation has been verified unless the generated audio was actually inspected.

## 11. Final Quality Checklist

Before submitting any script or generated voiceover, verify:

- [ ] The language is natural Egyptian Arabic, not formal Arabic.
- [ ] Word order sounds natural to Egyptians.
- [ ] Expressions such as "مصر كلها" are used where appropriate instead of unnatural constructions like "كل مصر".
- [ ] The opening captures attention quickly.
- [ ] The sentences are short, smooth, and easy to speak.
- [ ] The tone is confident, bold, clear, and human.
- [ ] The pacing and emotional intensity suit the content.
- [ ] Prices, numbers, names, and claims are accurate.
- [ ] The call to action is clear and natural.
- [ ] No unnecessary repetition, robotic phrasing, or forced slang remains.
- [ ] The script is ready to send to the selected voiceover tool.

**Final rule:** Natural Egyptian speech always takes priority over literal translation, formal Arabic grammar, and generic AI writing patterns. Every voiceover should sound as though a skilled Egyptian copywriter wrote it specifically for a real person to say aloud.
