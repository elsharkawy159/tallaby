# Salla seller experience: what to learn for Tallaby (F-59a)

Written 2026-11-03. Every source was read on that date. Public pages only: I did not create a Salla account or see its live dashboard, so screens are described as Salla's help articles describe them.

**Short answer:** Tallaby's setup order is fine and needs no change. Salla's useful lessons come after the design step: one screen for trust details, starter policy text, and a guided path to the first few orders.

## What I verified in the sources

**Signup (S1).** Email, mobile and password, then a code to the mobile. Next: legal entity (individual, establishment, company, charity), experience level, legal eligibility, product type, activity and sub-activity, then the plan page. S1 sets the eligibility rule as Saudi nationality or an investor residency, and age 18 or over.

**Setup order (S2).** Identity (name, description, logo, colour) → customer service number → domain → first product → pickup location on a map → store design → store verification. S2 opens with a short "prepare this first" list. The account's phone is used as the customer service number by default, and it shows in the store footer.

**Design (S3, S5).** The Wizard starts by choosing a simple or an advanced interface, and the seller can switch to advanced later. The simple route has three more steps:
- **Identity:** logo, description, colour and font.
- **Trust** (الموثوقية): return and exchange period, social links and, in Saudi Arabia only, a business certificate number.
- **Home page:** switch banners, customer reviews and store features on or off.

Salla also lets a seller upload their own font. S3 says the seller can leave at any time.

**Launch path (S4).** A guided task list in the dashboard, shown only to sellers with fewer than five paid orders. In S4's own words, it leads to "أول 5 مبيعات مدفوعة". Example tasks are adding at least one social account and installing a free WhatsApp button app. Progress shows on the home page and in the top bar. On reaching five, a congratulations notice appears and the tasks disappear. It can't be hidden before then, and some tasks need a paid plan.

**Social links (S4, S5).** They live in the design editor's footer settings and show at the bottom of the store. At least one customer service channel is required.

**Policies (S6).** Info pages have four types: general, privacy, return and exchange, and HTML. The two policy types open with a starter text the seller can edit. A return policy page also feeds a barcode on the invoice. The seller links pages from the store menu by hand.

**Shipping (S2, S7, S8).** One Shipping and Delivery page holds carriers, rates, COD settings and free shipping, with prices per carrier or region. The carrier list covers Salla's own waybills and app store carriers. Own couriers have a country, cities, a fee, a delivery time and a rate type (flat, by weight, by distance). S7 suggests a rate calculator and a page that tracks carrier quality.

**Payments and apps (S2, S9).** Online payment needs store verification and a Plus or Pro plan. The app store sells apps, some with monthly prices.

**Market (S9).** The home page says it is "مُصمّمة للتجّار في السعودية والإمارات". None of the nine pages I searched mentions Egypt.

## What I inferred (not stated by Salla)

- Salla doesn't look like a direct rival in Egypt today, given S1's eligibility rule and S9. So this is a source of ideas, not a threat. A dedicated Egypt offer could exist that I didn't find.
- Its signup is heavier because Saudi rules and payment verification need legal data. Tallaby can stay lighter.
- Salla's trust step and five-order path suggest its main problem is not finishing setup, but what to do between setup and first sales. No source gives figures for this.
- I couldn't confirm how the screens feel on a phone, or how many sellers use the simple route. The tutorials may show more than the articles do.

## Compared with Tallaby

Tallaby's live guide (`apps/dashboard/app/(main)/_components/setup-checklist.lib.ts`): **template → brand → first product → publish → shipping (later)**. The owner chose this order on purpose (`ROADMAP.md` log, 2026-11-01), and it stays.

| Topic | Salla | Tallaby today | Read |
|---|---|---|---|
| Signup | Legal and business questions, then a plan | Light signup, no plan choice | Tallaby is better for a small seller |
| First step | Identity, then product; design is sixth | Template first | Tallaby shows a real store sooner. Keep it |
| Brand | Inside the design flow, with a simple route | Website › Design, step 2 | Close. Salla's simple route is clearer |
| Shipping | In setup (pickup location); own page later | Last and optional | See the gap below |
| Policies | Period in the Wizard, starter text in pages | Settings text or a builder page; **not in the guide** | Salla is ahead |
| Social links | In the Wizard, and a launch path task | Website › Social; **not in the guide** | Salla is ahead |
| After setup | Path to five paid orders | The guide ends | Biggest gap |

**The `Roadmap_new.md` §7.1 journey** (theme, brand, shipping, return policy, social) has good steps but needs two changes:
- It leaves out product and publish, so it should sit around the live order, not replace it.
- Policy and social shouldn't be two separate steps. Salla's Wizard puts them on one screen.

Suggested journey: template → brand → product → publish → shipping (later) → **trust details (later): return period, social links, contact number.**

**A gap I found in Tallaby's code:** with shipping set up "later", a store checkout can still quote Tallaby's standard governorate rates (`packages/lib/src/shipping/store-quote.ts`, `tallaby_rates`). A seller may have orders at prices they never set. This matches the owner's "publish first" order, but the seller should be told. I read the code and didn't run a test order.

## Ideas worth adapting, ranked

Effort is my estimate: S = about a day, M = a few days.

1. **"Trust details" screen after publish. Effort: M.** One optional, later step: return period, social links, contact number. For COD-first sellers, a new store's main problem is whether a customer believes it. Keeps the owner's order and doesn't block publishing. Can reuse the existing Social page and the F-46 policy text, and join the F-44 prompts.
2. **First orders path. Effort: M.** After the guide ends, show practical tasks until the first five orders: share the store link, set up shipping, add a WhatsApp contact, make a coupon. Count delivered orders, not paid ones, because this is COD. Let the seller dismiss it, unlike Salla. Most signals can come from existing data, like the current guide.
3. **Starter return and shipping policy text. Effort: S.** A blank box stops sellers. Offer an Egyptian Arabic draft with the period left for the seller to fill in. The owner should approve the wording, and it must never promise a period for them.
4. **Shipping prompt that shows the current price. Effort: S.** The later step shows what customers are charged today and asks the seller to confirm or change it. Fixes the gap above without changing the order. Fits F-45a.
5. **"Prepare this first" list. Effort: S.** Name, logo if any, a product photo with its price, a WhatsApp number. Saves stop-and-start on a phone.
6. **Prefill the store contact number from signup, with confirmation. Effort: S.** Salla uses the account number by default. Tallaby should ask first, because this is often a personal number that would become public.
7. **Quick design route. Effort: M or more.** Logo, colour, font and home sections on or off, without opening the builder. Useful on phones, but check first whether Website › Design already does enough. Lowest priority.

## Things not to copy

- **Legal and eligibility questions at signup.** They come from Saudi rules and would lose sellers who just want a link.
- **A plan choice during setup, or tasks and product types locked behind a plan.** Tallaby's own plan work (F-55) keeps features the same on both plans.
- **Verification before accepting payment.** COD works from day one. That's a strength for Egyptian sellers.
- **A pickup map during setup.** Tallaby doesn't collect or deliver for sellers, and shipping stays "later".
- **Paid-order goals.** "Paid" means little when most orders are cash on delivery.
- **A path that can't be hidden.** The owner's F-44 rule is to be helpful without interrupting work.
- **The HTML page type and theme code editing.** `CLAUDE.md` rule 9 forbids seller code.
- **Shipping weight tiers, quality pages and carrier-count claims for now.** These fit a large carrier network, and a small seller doesn't need them yet.
- **Reordering Tallaby's setup to match Salla or §7.1.** The owner already decided this.
